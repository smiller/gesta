# Odes, 1.1

::: verse
Maecenas atavis edite regibus, | Maecenas, born of monarch ancestors,
o et praesidium et dulce decus meum, | The shield at once and glory of my life!
sunt quos curriculo pulverem Olympicum | There are who joy them in the Olympic strife
collegisse iuvat metaque fervidis | And love the dust they gather in the course;
evitata rotis palmaque nobilis | The goal by hot wheels shunned, the famous prize,
terrarum dominos evehit ad deos; | Exalt them to the gods that rule mankind;
hunc, si mobilium turba Quiritium | This joys, if rabbles fickle as the wind
certat tergeminis tollere honoribus; | Through triple grade of honours bid him rise,
illum, si proprio condidit horreo | That, if his granary has stored away
quicquid de Libycis verritur areis. | Of Libya’s thousand floors the yield entire;
Gaudentem patrios findere sarculo | The man who digs his field as did his sire,
agros Attalicis condicionibus | With honest pride, no Attalus may sway
numquam demoveas, ut trabe Cypria | By proffered wealth to tempt Myrtoan seas,
Myrtoum pavidus nauta secet mare. | The timorous captain of a Cyprian bark.
Luctantem Icariis fluctibus Africum | The winds that make Icarian billows dark
mercator metuens otium et oppidi | The merchant fears, and hugs the rural ease
laudat rura sui; mox reficit rates | Of his own village home; but soon, ashamed
quassas, indocilis pauperiem pati. | Of penury, he refits his battered craft.
Est qui nec veteris pocula Massici | There is, who thinks no scorn of Massic draught,
nec partem solido demere de die | Who robs the daylight of an hour unblamed,
spernit, nunc viridi membra sub arbuto | Now stretched beneath the arbute on the sward,
stratus, nunc ad aquae lene caput sacrae. | Now by some gentle river’s sacred spring;
Multos castra iuvant et lituo tubae | Some love the camp, the clarion’s joyous ring,
permixtus sonitus bellaque matribus | And battle, by the mother’s soul abhorred.
detestata. Manet sub Iove frigido | See, patient waiting in the clear keen air,
venator tenerae coniugis inmemor, | The hunter, thoughtless of his delicate bride,
seu visa est catulis cerva fidelibus, | Whether the trusty hounds a stag have eyed,
seu rupit teretis Marsus aper plagas. | Or the fierce Marsian boar has burst the snare.
Me doctarum hederae praemia frontium | To me the artist’s meed, the ivy wreath
dis miscent superis, me gelidum nemus | Is very heaven: me the sweet cool of woods,
Nympharumque leves cum Satyris chori | Where Satyrs frolic with the Nymphs, secludes
secernunt populo, si neque tibias | From rabble rout, so but Euterpe’s breath
Euterpe cohibet nec Polyhymnia | Fail not the flute, nor Polyhymnia fly
Lesboum refugit tendere barbiton. | Averse from stringing new the Lesbian lyre.
Quod si me lyricis vatibus inseres, | O, write my name among that minstrel choir,
sublimi feriam sidera vertice. | And my proud head shall strike upon the sky!
